Remote ID, in plain language

Remote ID is a broadcast. A cooperative aircraft tells nearby receivers who it is and where it is. Fusion Sensor puts that broadcast on a map. Aircraft that are not broadcasting, or that have Remote ID off, do not appear as Remote ID contacts.

What you can say

You can say what the sensors heard: a Remote ID track, radio energy on 2.4 or 5.8 GHz, ADS-B for manned traffic. Green is the client’s friendly list. Red is broadcasting off that list.

What you cannot say

You cannot say the air is empty of drones from Remote ID alone. Quiet is not a clearance. Analog FPV and some RID-off aircraft will not give you a cooperative identity.

If something goes wrong

The remote PIC reports to the FAA within ten days when there is serious injury, loss of consciousness, or property damage over $500 that is not the aircraft (14 CFR 107.9). 911 first. Then the file.

Module 2 · Monitoring fundamentals

How to run a listen

  1. Name the place and the hours. Put the laptop where it can see the sky and hear 2.4 / 5.8.
  2. Load the friendly list before you go live. Anything not on that list that broadcasts is red.
  3. Keep ADS-B on so manned traffic is on the same picture.
  4. When a red contact appears, hold it. Do not auto-clear. Write time, band, and who you told.
  5. Export the log at close. That is the record.

Hardware for Fusion Sensor

Laptop. ESP32-S3 Remote ID receiver. ADS-B 1090. SDR on 2.4 and 5.8 GHz. Antennas, USB, power. It detects. It does not jam.
