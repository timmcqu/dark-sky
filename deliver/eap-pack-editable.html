How this file sits with the Emergency Action Plan

OSHA requires a written emergency action plan for most workplaces. General industry is 29 CFR 1910.38. Construction is 29 CFR 1926.35. The employer writes and keeps that plan. This pack is the observation file operations and first-in companies keep with it: what the ground actually looks like, dated, with still numbers.

What 1910.38(c) and 1926.35 ask you to be able to show

Reporting a fire or other emergency. Evacuation and exit-route assignments. Accounting for people after evacuation. Rescue and medical duties. Names or titles of people who can explain the plan. Floor plans or workplace maps of escape routes (Subpart E appendix).

This pack gives you rows and stills for those facts. It does not replace the plan. It does not inspect, certify, or stamp.

How first-in uses it

Engine and medic companies size up from the stills: gates, pinch, the hole, confined entry, ambulance in, patient out. Fill what you see. Empty cells mean you have not looked yet.

Limits

Observation file for operations use with the employer’s Emergency Action Plan (29 CFR 1910.38 / 1926.35). Not an OSHA inspection. Not a PE stamp. Not a confined-space permit. Not a fire-code plan review. Dark Sky Systems LLC does not write the employer’s plan.
